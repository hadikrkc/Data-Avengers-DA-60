# Problem Analysis

## Project Topic
Budget vs Success
Does a bigger budget lead to better ratings and stronger box office results?

---

## 1. Proposed Project Statement

In the film industry, large production budgets are often seen as a sign of potential success. Studios spend millions on actors, visual effects, marketing, locations, and production quality with the expectation that these investments will lead to higher box office revenue and better audience ratings. However, this is not always the case. Some high budget movies fail to perform well, while some low budget movies become highly successful. This creates the main problem for this project: does a bigger budget actually lead to movie success?

---

## 2. Root Cause

The reason this problem exists is that movie success is influenced by many factors, not just budget.  
A movie may have a strong budget but a weak story, poor audience connection, bad reviews, or strong competition at the time of release.  

On the other hand, a low budget movie may succeed because of a strong storyline, emotional connection, positive word of mouth, good timing, or audience popularity.  

Therefore, budget may support success, but it may not be the only reason behind it.  
The root cause of the problem is the assumption that higher spending automatically creates better results.  

This project will test whether that assumption is valid by comparing budget with revenue and ratings. The analysis will help identify whether budget has a strong, weak, or limited relationship with movie success.

---

## 3. Collect Data

To analyze this problem, the project will use movie related data such as movie title, production budget, box office revenue, audience ratings, genre, and release year. This data will help compare movies with different budget levels and understand how they performed financially and critically.

---

## 4. Analyze the Impact

This problem is important because film studios and producers make major financial decisions based on expected success. If a high budget does not guarantee strong revenue or good ratings, then studios need to understand what other factors contribute to success.  

This analysis can help identify whether budget is truly a strong success factor or whether creative quality, audience response, genre, and timing play a bigger role.

---

## 5. Generate Analytical Approach

The project will compare high budget and low budget movies, observe revenue patterns, check audience rating trends, and identify exceptions where low budget movies performed better than expensive movies.  

It will also use correlation analysis and visualizations to understand the relationship between budget, ratings, and box office revenue.

---

## 6. Choose the Best Analysis Method

The best method for this project is to use exploratory data analysis, comparison charts, and correlation analysis. These methods will make it easier to see whether bigger budgets are connected to stronger box office results and better ratings, or whether the relationship is weak or inconsistent.

---

## 7. Expected Outcome

The expected outcome of this problem analysis is to understand whether budget is a major factor in movie success or only one of many contributing factors.  

The project may show that bigger budgets can help increase revenue potential, but they do not always guarantee better ratings or audience approval. This will provide a clearer view of how budget, revenue, and ratings are connected in the movie industry.
